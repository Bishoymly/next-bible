# Reformed gospel review rubric

## Scope

This is a correctness rubric for a short, public-facing gospel page, not a demand that the page become a miniature systematic theology. It uses both major branches of the historic Reformed confessional tradition: the Westminster Standards and the Three Forms of Unity. The sources are the confessional texts themselves on official denominational sites.

The governing distinction is:

- **Essential to the page's message:** the identity and saving work of Christ, humanity's guilt and need, Christ's death and resurrection, salvation by grace received through faith, repentance, and a sincere invitation to come to Christ.
- **Reformed guardrails:** faith and repentance are grace-enabled rather than autonomous human contributions; justification rests wholly on Christ's righteousness; changed life is fruit and evidence, never the price of acceptance.
- **Usually unnecessary on a concise gospel page:** election and reprobation, the extent of the atonement, covenant formulations, baptismal debates, church polity, and other intramural Reformed distinctives. Omitting these is not doctrinal error.

## Doctrinal rubric

| God and Christ | Jesus is not merely a helper or example: he is the eternal Son who truly became human, one person with divine and human natures, and the only mediator. | [Westminster Shorter Catechism (WSC) 6, 21–22](https://opc.org/sc.html); [Westminster Confession of Faith (WCF) 8.2](https://opc.org/wcf.html#Chapter_08); [Belgic Confession (BC) 8–11, 18–19](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/belgic-confession) |
| Human need | Sin is guilt and corruption before the holy God, bringing judgment and death; fallen people cannot repair or prepare themselves for salvation apart from regenerating grace. | [WSC 16–19](https://opc.org/sc.html); [WCF 6.2–6.4; 9.3](https://opc.org/wcf.html#Chapter_06); [Canons of Dort (CD) III/IV.1–3](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/canons-dort?language=en) |

| Grace and justification | God justifies freely for Christ's sake alone, crediting Christ's righteousness rather than any work, decision, prayer, or moral improvement of ours. | Faith | Saving faith includes knowledge of God's promise and personal, wholehearted trust in Christ and his merits. “Accept Jesus” can carry this meaning, but “receive and rest on Christ” or “trust in Christ” is less liable to sound like a saving work or bare decision. | [WCF 14.1–14.3](https://opc.org/wcf.html#Chapter_14); [WSC 86](https://opc.org/sc.html); [HC 20–21](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism) |
| Repentance | The gospel calls sinners to turn from sin to God with sorrow and hatred for sin and a new desire for obedience. | New life | A living faith necessarily bears the fruit of love, obedience, and ongoing warfare against sin, but these works follow justification and never become its ground. Avoid both legalism and a faith with no call to discipleship. | [WCF 11.2; 13.1–13.3; 16.2, 16.5](https://opc.org/wcf.html#Chapter_11); [HC 86–91](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism); [BC 24](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/belgic-confession) |
| Invitation | The promise of eternal life in Christ and the command to repent and believe should be announced freely and sincerely to all hearers. Election need not be explained before inviting anyone to Christ. | [CD I.2–5; II.5; III/IV.8–9](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/canons-dort?language=en); [WSC 31](https://opc.org/sc.html) |
| Assurance and pastoral tone | Christ, not the intensity of a decision or the wording of a prayer, is the believer's comfort. Assurance is real but may be weak, delayed, or shaken, so avoid promising certainty merely because someone repeated a formula. | [HC 1, 21](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism); [WCF 18.1–18.4](https://opc.org/wcf.html#Chapter_18); [CD V.9–11](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/canons-dort?language=en) |
| Church and sacraments | Direct new believers toward a faithful church, baptism, the Lord's Supper, the Word, and prayer as Christ-appointed means by which faith is nourished. Do not present any of them as extra grounds of justification. | [WCF 25.1–25.3; 27.1, 27.3](https://opc.org/wcf.html#Chapter_25); [HC 65–67](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism); [BC 27–29, 33–35](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/belgic-confession) |

## Page-sensitive cautions

Applied to common evangelistic wording, the confessions support these measured judgments:

- “God loves you and has a plan for your life” is not false in itself, but it is too indefinite to carry the gospel. It should not displace God's holiness, human guilt, Christ's accomplishment, and the promise of reconciliation and eternal life ([CD I.1–4](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/canons-dort?language=en)).
- “Follow these steps” is usable only as an editorial outline. If it sounds causal or meritorious, it conflicts with the confession that the Spirit enables sinners to embrace the Christ freely offered in the gospel ([WSC 29–31](https://opc.org/sc.html)).
- “Invite Jesus into your heart” and “accept Jesus as personal Savior” are not confessional formulas. They can be used charitably if defined as trusting, receiving, and resting on Christ, but those clearer verbs better protect the object and instrument of faith ([WCF 14.2](https://opc.org/wcf.html#Chapter_14); [HC 20–21](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism)).
- A model prayer may express repentance and faith, but the prayer must not be presented as the mechanism or guarantee of salvation. The ground is Christ alone and the instrument is faith alone ([WCF 11.1–11.2](https://opc.org/wcf.html#Chapter_11); [BC 22–23](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/belgic-confession)).
- Do not turn a broad Christian gospel page into a test of every Reformed distinctive. The most faithful emphasis is God's sovereign grace in the crucified and risen Christ, freely offered to sinners and received through Spirit-given faith, which produces repentance and a new life ([CD I.2–5; III/IV.10–12](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/canons-dort?language=en); [WCF 11.1–11.2; 13.1](https://opc.org/wcf.html#Chapter_11)).
